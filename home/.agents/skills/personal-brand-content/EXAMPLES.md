# Examples

These show how to adapt the same core insight across TikTok and LinkedIn using the brand voice, hook rules, and platform specs.

---

## Example 1: The 1,200 Applications (Story Phase)

### TikTok Script (60-75 seconds)

**Hook (on-screen text: "1200 JOBS. 0 EASY WINS.")**
I applied to 1,200 jobs before I understood how hiring really works.

**Body**
I was an international student in the UK, working side jobs, sending out applications like it was a full-time job. I thought volume would win. It didn't. I got rejections from the UK, Oman, Dubai, and Mumbai. What I didn't build was visibility. I had no LinkedIn presence, no proof of work, and no warm conversations before hitting apply.

**Close**
If I had to start again, I would focus on three things first: a sharper LinkedIn presence, proof of work people can see, and warmer conversations before I ever click apply.

**CTA**
What is one thing you would do differently if you started your job search again?

---

### LinkedIn Post

**Hook**
I applied to 1,200 jobs and got nowhere.

Not because I was unqualified. Because I was invisible.

**Body**
For three years I treated applications like a numbers game. I sent out resumes to the UK, Oman, Dubai, and Mumbai. I worked hospitality jobs on the side to stay afloat.

What I didn't do:
- Build a LinkedIn presence that showed how I thought
- Create proof of work people could find without asking
- Have warm conversations before I needed something

Visibility and positioning do the heavy lifting. Applications are just the final step.

**CTA**
If you had to start your job search again today, what would you focus on first?

## Example 2: Over-Preparing for the Interview (Lesson Phase)

### TikTok Script (60 seconds)

**Hook (on-screen text: "I WALKED IN PREPARED.")**
I got my first internship because I overprepared on purpose.

**Body**
I had no experience. No connections. Just a marketing degree and side jobs. For this social media internship, I didn't just research the company. I built a six-month social media plan and walked the interviewer through it. She looked at me and said, "You definitely get brownie points for this."

Most people prepare to answer questions. I prepared to solve a problem.

**Close**
Before interviews now, I still ask: what would I do in the first 30, 60, and 90 days?

**CTA**
What is the most overprepared you have ever been for an opportunity?

---

### LinkedIn Post (with Carousel Outline)

**Hook**
Most people prepare to answer interview questions.

I prepared to solve a problem.

That is how I got my first internship with zero connections and zero experience.

**Body**
For a social media internship at a homelessness charity, I walked in with a full six-month plan. The interviewer told me on the spot I had earned "brownie points."

The lesson: initiative that is visible beats credentials that are only on paper.

If you want to stand out, do the work before the job.

**Carousel Outline**
1. Cover: "Do the work before the job."
2. The situation: no experience, no network
3. The move: built a 6-month plan instead of just answering questions
4. The outcome: got the job on the spot
5. The framework: 30-60-90 day thinking before you interview
6. CTA: What is one time initiative changed your outcome?

## Example 3: Boredom as Feedback (Framework Phase)

### TikTok Script (75 seconds)

**Hook (on-screen text: "BOREDOM = A SIGNAL.")**
I got bored doing repetitive SEO work. That boredom saved my career.

**Body**
At Red Bull I had access to millions of data points. I should have been excited. Instead, I was doing on-page optimization over and over. I felt guilty for being bored at a dream company. Then I realized: boredom is data. It tells you what kind of work drains you. For me, repetitive execution without insight was the drain.

So I asked: is the problem the role, or the level of challenge? It was the challenge. That is when I moved into data analytics, technical SEO training, and eventually CRM.

**Close**
If your role feels repetitive, treat boredom like feedback. It is telling you where to grow next.

**CTA**
What is one thing boredom has taught you about what you actually want?

---

### LinkedIn Post

**Hook**
Boredom is not a character flaw. It is career data.

**Body**
I was at Red Bull Media House with access to millions of data points, and I was bored.

Not because the company was bad. Because repetitive on-page optimization was draining me. I felt guilty. Then I reframed it.

Boredom tells you one of two things:
1. The role lacks challenge
2. You have outgrown the work

For me, it was both. That signal pushed me into technical SEO training, a masters in data analytics, and eventually CRM and influencer marketing analytics.

If you feel bored, do not ignore it. Interrogate it.

**CTA**
Has boredom ever pushed you toward a better career move?

## Example 4: Repurposing a Framework (Cross-Platform)

**Core insight:** The best marketing work lives between channels, not inside one dashboard.

### TikTok Version
**Hook:** "SEO + PPC TOGETHER."
At my agency internship, I stopped reporting SEO and PPC separately. I built one measurement framework. That is when the real insights showed up.

**Body:** Most teams keep channels in silos. I connected the data. We could finally see incremental growth and cross-channel effects. One dashboard. One story.

**CTA:** Are your channels talking to each other or ignoring each other?

### LinkedIn Version
**Hook:** "The best marketing work lives between channels, not inside one dashboard."

**Body:** At Pixel Kicks, I built integrated measurement frameworks combining SEO and PPC data. Before that, the channels were reported in silos. After, we could track incremental growth and cross-channel effects.

Your best insights usually live in the gaps.

**CTA:** How do you connect channel data in your current role?

### Carousel Version
1. Cover: "Your best insights live in the gaps."
2. The problem: SEO and PPC reported separately
3. The blindspot: no one saw cross-channel impact
4. The solution: one integrated measurement framework
5. The outcome: clearer incremental growth tracking
6. The takeaway: look between dashboards, not just inside them
7. CTA: How do you connect your channels?

## Example 5: Immigrant Journey / Sponsorship (Story + Framework)

### TikTok Script

**Hook (on-screen text: "SPONSORSHIP IS BUILT.")**
It took me 8 years and 1,200 applications to get sponsored. It was not luck.

**Body**
I came to the UK in 2016 as an undergrad from Oman. I worked hospitality jobs. I taught myself SEO in my dorm room during COVID. I did unpaid internships. I worked two jobs and burned out. Every step built proof.

When I finally walked into a B2B manufacturer, I wasn't just asking for a visa. I was bringing 8 years of stacked skills: social media, SEO, analytics, CRM, dashboards, technical writing, sales, and agency work.

**Close**
Sponsorship is built, not lucky. Build proof before you ask for the door to open.

**CTA**
If you are building toward sponsorship, what skill are you stacking right now?

---

### LinkedIn Post

**Hook**
I got sponsored after 8 years, not 8 weeks.

**Body**
1,200 applications. Two countries of side jobs. A masters degree. Four BrightonSEO events. A speaking gig. A podcast I messaged my way into.

When I finally got my Skilled Worker visa, it looked like luck. It wasn't.

It was proof stacked over time:
- Social media and storytelling
- SEO and technical audits
- CRM, propensity modelling, and dashboards
- Agency deliverables and client communication
- Sales floors and commercial instinct

Sponsorship is not a lottery ticket. It is the result of becoming hard to ignore.

**CTA**
For the international professionals building toward sponsorship: what proof are you stacking this year?
